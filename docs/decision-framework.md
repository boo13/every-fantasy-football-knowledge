# A repeatable decision framework

These are research heuristics, not empirically proven predictions from this repository. Reviewed 2026-08-31; update when contrary evidence or format differences matter.

## First establish the decision

Get scoring, starting slots, league size, format, eligible options, deadline, and relevant constraints privately. A one-QB redraft decision is not a superflex dynasty decision. A public trend cannot tell you whether a player is available in your league.

## Then gather evidence

| Question | Evidence to seek | Common mistake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can the player play? | Official injury/game-status report, inactive list, transaction | Treating a blank provider flag as cleared |
| What is the expected role? | Recent snaps, routes, carries, targets, competition, coaching statements | Treating depth-chart order as guaranteed workload |
| What did they actually do? | Same-season, same-time-window stats and game context | Mixing a career total with a recent sample |
| What is the opportunity cost? | Available alternatives, replacement level, roster needs | Ranking by raw points across unlike positions |
| How uncertain is the forecast? | Multiple dated projections, role uncertainty, range of outcomes | Inventing a precise probability from a narrative |
| What would change the answer? | Teammate availability, starting QB, weather, scoring differences | Presenting conditional advice as unconditional |

The distinction between role and outcome is our analytical approach, not a conclusion supplied by the raw data. Sources for recorded fields: [nflverse](https://github.com/nflverse/nflverse-data); configuration examples: [Sleeper scoring](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/3998131-what-scoring-options-are-available).

## Draft

Use a dated, format-matched ADP/projection source, compare positional tiers against replacement options, and assess roster construction. Historical points are background evidence, not next-season predictions. Avoid an inflexible round-by-round script; availability changes the decision. Evaluate rookies separately from players with NFL history.

## Waivers

Investigate why attention or opportunity changed, whether it may persist, and what you would give up. Check upcoming byes and the actual waiver deadline privately. Express budget suggestions as conditional percentages of remaining FAAB only when rules and alternatives are known; never place bids from this shared repository.

## Start/sit

Compare eligible options under identical scoring and timing assumptions. Recheck availability near kickoff. Preserve late-game flexibility where the platform allows it; verify lineup lock rules before moving anyone. State which unresolved news could reverse the recommendation.

## Trades

Compare expected future contribution and replacement options, not just season-to-date points. Account for role, injury uncertainty, bye timing, positional needs and format. Do not judge real colleagues, infer their intentions, or store private negotiations here.

## Learn without hindsight

Write a hypothesis before the relevant games with an evidence cutoff, scope, measurable outcome, and review date. Later record the actual outcome and confounders. A single correct result does not validate a method, and a reasonable process can lose one week. Repeated evidence may justify a conditional durable lesson; contradicted claims need dated corrections, not silent deletion.
