# Fantasy football handbook

Reviewed 2026-08-31. These guides explain how to reason, not which players to select today. All numerical examples and lettered players are hypothetical. No example describes an actual league or person.

New to the sport? Read [football and fantasy in one sitting](../football-and-fantasy.md), then [scoring and formats](scoring-and-formats.md). An LLM should first follow [START_HERE.md](../../START_HERE.md), including the privacy boundaries and freshness checks.

## Three kinds of statements

- **Rules and definitions:** supported by original NFL, platform, or methodology documentation. A platform example is not the rule of every league.
- **Analytical heuristics:** the library's conditional reasoning tools. These are not validated predictive findings from this repository.
- **Hypothetical calculations:** explain arithmetic under stated assumptions. The assumed projections, probabilities, and prices are not empirical estimates.

Sources are cited near their claims. A reviewed date means the public page was checked, not that an older platform article was rewritten that day. If a platform setting, model, data feed, or NFL policy changes, revisit the relevant passage.

## How to use this without ingesting everything

Read the relevant chapter plus [the decision framework](../decision-framework.md). Then open only the dated [current evidence](../../context/CURRENT.md), [research](../../research/LATEST.md), and player/game evidence needed for the question. The [source registry](../../SOURCES.md) is authoritative about what the collector actually supplies.

A useful answer identifies its evidence cutoff, league assumptions, available alternatives, source dates, confidence limits, and the event that would change the recommendation. Obtain league-specific inputs privately; never save private rosters, budgets, negotiations, or transaction plans in this public library.

## Turn reading into cumulative learning

For a public football hypothesis, record the expected role or usage **before** the games, then review both the usage and the result. Distinguish a correct forecast from a favorable outcome that occurred for an unexpected reason. Preserve contradictory evidence. Follow the [weekly runbook](../weekly-runbook.md) and [durable-learning requirements](../solutions/README.md); do not promote a handbook heuristic to an established lesson merely because it sounds plausible.

For example: “If the publicly reported starting role persists, Player A's route participation should remain above the stated baseline over the next two complete games.” State how routes and dropbacks will be measured, where they are available, what would disprove the claim, and what an injury would do to the interpretation. If those data cannot be obtained, keep the question unresolved.
