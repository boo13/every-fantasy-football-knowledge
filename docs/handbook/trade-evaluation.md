# Trade evaluation: compare complete lineups

Reviewed 2026-08-31. Valuation procedures are analytical heuristics. All players and projections below are fictional; no actual negotiations belong in this repository.

## Begin with the question the trade is solving

Ask privately for scoring, starting slots, player availability, the remaining schedule, trade-processing rules, and whether the decision is redraft, keeper, or dynasty. The same exchange can improve both rosters because replacement options and needs differ.

Do not evaluate a trade by adding season-to-date totals or the numbers in a trade calculator. Points already earned remain in the past. A calculator's units are a model or market estimate; they are not automatically additive, transferable between formats, or calibrated to a specific roster.

## Compare before and after, including the extra slot

For a two-for-one exchange, the team receiving one player also gains a roster slot. The team receiving two may need to drop someone. Calculate actual starting-lineup changes before pricing speculative depth.

Hypothetical example with one WR slot and one FLEX affected:

| Roster state | WR projection | FLEX projection | Affected starter total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Before | Player A: 14 | Player B: 12 | 26 |
| Trade A and B for C; promote bench D | Player C: 18 | Player D: 9 | 27 |

The package is not “26 versus 18.” The starting-lineup comparison is 26 versus 27 under these assumed projections. The vacated bench spot could add further contingent value, but only if a useful free agent exists. If D projects for 5 instead of 9, the post-trade total is 23 and the conclusion reverses.

Now test the deal across bye weeks, injuries, and roster constraints rather than treating a single idealized week as the season. A trade that gains one point in healthy weeks but creates an uncovered position may not be an improvement.

## A transparent evaluation worksheet

| Dimension | Evidence or calculation | Warning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Expected starter improvement | Same-source, same-date projections with actual replacements | Adding bench points as if they always score |
| Useful weeks | Remaining relevant games, byes, eligibility | Counting NFL postseason games outside the fantasy season |
| Availability and role | Official reports plus current usage | Treating return from absence as full workload |
| Depth | Who covers a plausible absence after the trade? | Giving every backup full starter value |
| Timing | Acceptance, review, processing, and player lock times | Assuming an accepted player can start immediately |
| Retention | Keeper cost, future rights, roster horizon | Importing dynasty value into redraft |
| Alternatives | Free agents or a smaller exchange | Ignoring a nearly free way to solve the same problem |

An approximate rest-of-season calculation can sum the expected difference between the best legal lineup before and after for each relevant week. This is better specified than a raw player sum, but projections remain uncertain, and future injuries or transactions may change both lineups.

## Sensitivity is more useful than false precision

Run at least a base case and a plausible unfavorable case. Examples: a player's role remains limited; a returning teammate reduces targets; the newly opened roster spot produces no useful addition. If the exchange is only attractive under optimistic assumptions, say so.

Do not describe players as certain “buy lows” or “sell highs” solely because points differ from a recent average. Review role, opportunity, and scoring. A decline may be meaningful; a spike may reflect a real new job. The [uncertainty guide](opportunity-and-uncertainty.md) explains why regression is not a repayment schedule.

## Deadline and processing rules are part of value

Sleeper's current football documentation ties the chosen trade-deadline week to its final game's conclusion, and describes treatment of accepted trades still in review. That is a platform rule, not the NFL's transaction deadline or a universal fantasy rule. [Trade deadline](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/2435411-when-is-my-trade-deadline).

Its weekend-trade documentation also distinguishes players who already played from those who have not when processing occurs. Timing can make an otherwise balanced exchange affect the current week unevenly. Confirm the setting and effective lineup before agreeing. [Weekend processing](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/3351190-how-could-a-weekend-trade-impact-my-team).

Keeper and dynasty exchanges additionally require future pick year, round, likely range, retention rules, and roster cost. A future first-round pick is not a known player or guaranteed early selection. See [dynasty and keepers](dynasty-and-keepers.md).

## Fair analysis does not require judging people

Describe each side's football tradeoffs without inventing motives, personal weaknesses, or private information. Do not use company relationships, social pressure, collusion, deceptive injury claims, or uninformed-manager targeting as strategy. Differences in valuation are normal; suspected rule violations belong with the league's agreed process, not an LLM's unsupported accusation.

## LLM output contract

Show before/after starters, the necessary add/drop consequences, base and adverse scenarios, the effect of processing time, and an explicit uncertainty statement. A recommendation should read “favorable if these assumptions hold,” not “wins the trade by 17.3 points” without a justified model. Keep offers and negotiations private. Public learning should concern forecast quality, not scorekeeping about individual managers.
